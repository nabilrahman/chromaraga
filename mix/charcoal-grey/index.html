Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 3 parts Ivory black 75%
  • 1 part Titanium white 25%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 3 parts of ivory black. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of titanium white, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 2 parts Ivory black 67%
  • 1 part Titanium white 33%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 2 parts of ivory black. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of titanium white, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing greys, blacks and off-whites

  • Greys mixed from complements, such as ultramarine and burnt sienna, look livelier than black and white, and you can tilt them warm or cool by changing the ratio.
  • Ivory black with white gives a slightly cool, bluish grey. Add a touch of burnt sienna or yellow ochre to warm it.
