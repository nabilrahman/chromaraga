How to mix lemon yellow

#F2E14C

A cool, slightly greenish yellow. A bright yellow cannot be mixed from other paints, so it starts from a tube.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 3 parts Lemon yellow (Hansa) 75%
  • 1 part Titanium white 25%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 3 parts of lemon yellow (Hansa). A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of titanium white, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 2 parts Lemon yellow (Hansa) 67%
  • 1 part Titanium white 33%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 2 parts of lemon yellow (Hansa). A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of titanium white, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 1 part Lemon yellow (Hansa) 100%

Close match. Thinned to a strong mix with little water.

  1. Use lemon yellow (Hansa) straight from the tube as a strong mix with little water.
  2. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  3. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing yellows

  • Yellow is weak and easy to dirty. Keep brushes clean and add small amounts of other colours to the yellow.
  • Darkening yellow with black turns it olive green. Use a touch of brown or violet instead.
