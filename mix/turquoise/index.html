How to mix turquoise

#33B3A6

A bright, light blue-green named after the stone. It needs clean pigments: any red or brown in the mix will grey it.

Turquoise in acrylic

Buy the paints for this mix

No single acrylic paint comes close to turquoise, so mixing is the way to get it. You need these:

Titanium white PW6BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Lemon yellow (Hansa) PY3BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Phthalo blue (green shade) PB15:3BlickJackson's ArtAmazon

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 12 parts Titanium white 80%
  • 2 parts Lemon yellow (Hansa) 13%
  • 1 part Phthalo blue (green shade) 7%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 12 parts of titanium white. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 2 parts of lemon yellow (Hansa), and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of phthalo blue (green shade), a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Turquoise in oil

Buy the paints for this mix

No single oil paint comes close to turquoise, so mixing is the way to get it. You need these:

Titanium white PW6BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Phthalo blue (green shade) PB15:3BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Cadmium yellow medium PY35BlickJackson's ArtAmazon

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 29 parts Titanium white 92%
  • 1½ parts Phthalo blue (green shade) 5%
  • 1 part Cadmium yellow medium 3%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 29 parts of titanium white. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1½ parts of phthalo blue (green shade), and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of cadmium yellow medium, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 1 part Phthalo blue (green shade) 50%
  • 1 part Lemon yellow (Hansa) 50%

Very close match. Thinned to a medium wash.

  1. Put out 1 part of phthalo blue (green shade). A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of lemon yellow (Hansa), a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Thin the mix with water to a medium wash.
  4. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing greens

  • Yellow and blue make green. A cool, greenish yellow with phthalo blue gives the brightest greens; ultramarine, which leans violet, gives duller, olive-leaning greens.
  • To dull a green, add a little red or a brown. To darken it, add blue or a dark earth colour rather than black, which tends to make greens muddy.
