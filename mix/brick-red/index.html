Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 3 parts Burnt sienna 75%
  • 1 part Cadmium red light 25%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 3 parts of burnt sienna. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of cadmium red light, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 6 parts Lemon yellow (Hansa) 67%
  • 2 parts Quinacridone magenta 22%
  • 1 part Burnt sienna 11%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 6 parts of lemon yellow (Hansa). A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 2 parts of quinacridone magenta, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of burnt sienna, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 8 parts Burnt sienna 62%
  • 4 parts Quinacridone magenta 31%
  • 1 part Ivory black 8%

Very close match. Thinned to a strong mix with little water.

  1. Put out 8 parts of burnt sienna. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 4 parts of quinacridone magenta, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of ivory black, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Thin the mix with water to a strong mix with little water.
  5. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  6. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing reds and oranges

  • A bright red is hard to mix from other paints, so most painters buy one warm red and one cool red and shift between them.
  • To darken a red, add a touch of blue or a deep red such as alizarin crimson rather than black, which turns red brownish grey.
