How to mix navy blue

#1D2A55

A very dark blue. Ultramarine darkened with a little of a warm colour, such as a red or burnt sienna, stays blue, where black can turn it grey.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 8 parts French ultramarine 89%
  • 1 part Cadmium red light 11%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 8 parts of French ultramarine. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of cadmium red light, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 18 parts French ultramarine 78%

  • 1 part Cadmium red light 4%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 18 parts of French ultramarine. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 4 parts of yellow ochre, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of cadmium red light, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 4 parts French ultramarine 80%
  • 1 part Ivory black 20%

Very close match. Thinned to a strong mix with little water.

  1. Put out 4 parts of French ultramarine. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of ivory black, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Thin the mix with water to a strong mix with little water.
  4. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing blues

  • Blues are strong, phthalo blue especially. Add the blue to the lighter colour in very small amounts, never the other way round.
  • Ultramarine leans violet and phthalo blue leans green. Start from the blue that already leans the way you need.
