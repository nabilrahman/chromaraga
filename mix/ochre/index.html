Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 1 part Lemon yellow (Hansa) 50%
  • 1 part Yellow ochre 50%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 1 part of lemon yellow (Hansa). A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of yellow ochre, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 4 parts Cadmium yellow medium 50%
  • 3 parts Burnt sienna 38%
  • 1 part French ultramarine 13%

Near-exact match. Thinned to a strong mix with little water.

  1. Put out 4 parts of cadmium yellow medium. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 3 parts of burnt sienna, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of French ultramarine, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Thin the mix with water to a strong mix with little water.
  5. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  6. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing yellows

  • Yellow is weak and easy to dirty. Keep brushes clean and add small amounts of other colours to the yellow.
  • Darkening yellow with black turns it olive green. Use a touch of brown or violet instead.
