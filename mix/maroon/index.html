Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 28 parts Quinacridone magenta 65%
  • 14 parts Burnt sienna 33%
  • 1 part Ivory black 2%

Very close match.

  1. Put out 28 parts of quinacridone magenta. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 14 parts of burnt sienna, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of ivory black, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 30 parts Quinacridone magenta 66%
  • 15 parts Burnt sienna 33%
  • ½ part Ivory black 1%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 30 parts of quinacridone magenta. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 15 parts of burnt sienna, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add ½ part of ivory black, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 4 parts Quinacridone magenta 80%
  • 1 part Ivory black 20%

Close match. Thinned to a strong mix with little water.

  1. Put out 4 parts of quinacridone magenta. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of ivory black, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Thin the mix with water to a strong mix with little water.
  4. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing reds and oranges

  • A bright red is hard to mix from other paints, so most painters buy one warm red and one cool red and shift between them.
  • To darken a red, add a touch of blue or a deep red such as alizarin crimson rather than black, which turns red brownish grey.
