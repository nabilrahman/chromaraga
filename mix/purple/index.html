How to mix purple

#6B3F8F

Most disappointing purples come from the wrong red and blue. A violet-leaning blue and a blue-leaning red keep it clean.

Purple in acrylic

Buy the paints for this mix

No single acrylic paint comes close to purple, so mixing is the way to get it. You need these:

Quinacridone magenta PR122BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Titanium white PW6BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
French ultramarine PB29BlickJackson's ArtAmazon

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 3 parts Quinacridone magenta 50%
  • 2 parts Titanium white 33%
  • 1 part French ultramarine 17%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 3 parts of quinacridone magenta. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 2 parts of titanium white, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of French ultramarine, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Near-exact match.

  2. Add 3 parts of titanium white, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of French ultramarine, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Purple in watercolour

Buy the paints for this mix

No single watercolour paint comes close to purple, so mixing is the way to get it. You need these:

Quinacridone magenta PR122BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Lemon yellow (Hansa) PY3BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Phthalo blue (green shade) PB15:3BlickJackson's ArtAmazon

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 8 parts Quinacridone magenta 67%
  • 3 parts Lemon yellow (Hansa) 25%
  • 1 part Phthalo blue (green shade) 8%

Near-exact match. Thinned to a strong mix with little water.

  1. Put out 8 parts of quinacridone magenta. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 3 parts of lemon yellow (Hansa), and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of phthalo blue (green shade), a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Thin the mix with water to a strong mix with little water.
  5. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  6. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing purples

  • Mixed purples go dull when the red leans orange or the blue leans green. Use a blue that leans violet, such as ultramarine, with a red that leans blue, such as quinacridone magenta.
  • Mix the purple first, then add it to white a little at a time for lighter versions.
