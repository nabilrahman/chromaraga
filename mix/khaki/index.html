How to mix khaki

#B5A77A

A dull yellowish brown with a hint of green, the colour of the uniform cloth.

Khaki in acrylic

Buy it ready-made

Yellow ochre PY43

Close straight from the tube.

BlickJackson's ArtAmazon

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 30 parts Titanium white 86%
  • 4 parts Cadmium yellow medium 11%
  • 1 part Ivory black 3%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 30 parts of titanium white. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 4 parts of cadmium yellow medium, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of ivory black, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Khaki in oil

Buy the paints for this mix

No single oil paint comes close to khaki, so mixing is the way to get it. You need these:

Titanium white PW6BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
Yellow ochre PY43BlickJackson's ArtAmazon
French ultramarine PB29BlickJackson's ArtAmazon

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 29 parts Titanium white 66%
  • 14 parts Yellow ochre 32%
  • 1 part French ultramarine 2%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 29 parts of titanium white. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 14 parts of yellow ochre, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of French ultramarine, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 4 parts Ivory black 80%
  • 1 part Cadmium yellow medium 20%

Very close match. Thinned to a pale wash with plenty of water.

  1. Put out 4 parts of ivory black. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of cadmium yellow medium, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Thin the mix with water to a pale wash with plenty of water.
  4. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  5. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing browns

  • Browns come from mixing complements: red and green, blue and orange, or all three primaries. Change the ratio to tilt a brown warmer or cooler.
  • Burnt sienna, burnt umber and raw umber are cheap, lightfast and save a lot of mixing.
