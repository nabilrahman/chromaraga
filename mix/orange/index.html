Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 20 parts Lemon yellow (Hansa) 95%
  • 1 part Quinacridone magenta 5%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 20 parts of lemon yellow (Hansa). A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of quinacridone magenta, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Acrylic dries a little darker than it looks wet, so let a test dab dry before you mix a large batch.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 6 parts Cadmium yellow medium 86%
  • 1 part Quinacridone magenta 14%

Near-exact match.

  1. Put out 6 parts of cadmium yellow medium. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 1 part of quinacridone magenta, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Mix with a palette knife rather than a brush: it keeps the colour clean and wastes less paint.
  4. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Mix it from 10 basic paints

  • 26 parts Burnt sienna 72%
  • 9 parts Cadmium yellow medium 25%
  • 1 part French ultramarine 3%

Near-exact match. Thinned to a strong mix with little water.

  1. Put out 26 parts of burnt sienna. A part is any equal measure: a pea-sized blob, a knife tip or a brushful.
  2. Add 9 parts of cadmium yellow medium, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  3. Add 1 part of French ultramarine, a little at a time, and mix it in completely before you judge the colour.
  4. Thin the mix with water to a strong mix with little water.
  5. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Mix a little stronger than you want and test on a scrap of the same paper.
  6. Put a dab next to your reference and squint: if it looks too light, too dark or too bright, adjust in small steps.

Tips for mixing reds and oranges

  • A warm yellow and a warm red make the cleanest oranges. A cool red such as magenta makes a duller, browner orange.
  • Earthy oranges like terracotta and rust are much easier starting from burnt sienna.
